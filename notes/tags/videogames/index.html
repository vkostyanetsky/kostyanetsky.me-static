ChatGPT Mimicry
29 September 2024 ·

Colleagues are actively experimenting with o1-preview from OpenAI: the model turned out to be genuinely interesting. Unfortunately, tasks for it don’t come up too often — regular 4o handles most day-to-day stuff just fine. Bug-finding in code, analyzing medical tests in an unfamiliar language, or trying to recall the name of a childhood book with only a vague recollection of the plot — I can’t easily think of anything it wouldn’t handle.

It’s always fascinating to see how the AI reasons, jokes, and generally tries to act human. I’d say it won’t pass the Turing test just yet, but every now and then, it comes eerily close.

The answer below made me smile. Even leaving philosophy aside — looks like Skynet is off the table! :) Though, I’m afraid the future from the SOMA ending is still possible.

Clementine Will Remember That
9 August 2024 ·

When ChatGPT remembers a detail from a conversation for the future, it displays a "Memory updated" badge above its response. Like, got it:

Every time I see it, it makes me smile because it instantly reminds me of The Walking Dead and the meme “Clementine will remember that”. In that game, the characters surrounding the protagonist would remember his decisions, and it would influence their behavior. This included Clementine — a girl the main character saved at the beginning of the game.

In the game, this mechanic was honestly implemented in a rather mediocre way, but when it comes to ChatGPT — it works. The attention to detail is so strong that sometimes I have to remind myself that I’m talking to a language model, not an extraordinarily attentive and very knowledgeable person.

Black Mesa
13 April 2020 ·

They’ve given the legendary, but now pretty creaky, Half-Life a careful makeover and thrown in a bunch of new ideas. It turned out great; you can really see the fans put their hearts into it :-) Xen benefited especially, although I think the factory could have been shorter. Still, the reworked Nihilanth fight more than makes up for all the suffering.

There are minor issues with music, optimization, and translation, but nothing particularly serious. Given how long it was in development, it’s amazing they got it out at all. Long projects tend to wear people out and burn them out, and that’s before you even get to the enormous job of bringing a twenty-year-old game up to date! Quite a nasty piece of work you managed over there, guys. I am impressed.

Control
14 September 2019 ·

I have two complaints about this game. First, its Altered Items: their descriptions feel a bit tame next to the SCP Foundation writers’ wild ideas. There are some pleasant exceptions, but this part still feels weaker than the rest of the game’s paranormal material.

My second gripe is where they chose to play My Dark Disquet by Poets of the Fall. Damn it, that track is far too good for such an unremarkable section! I expected it during the credits or some crazy fight.

I know both complaints sound a bit silly, but that really is all I’ve got. Control looks fantastic, plays brilliantly, and tells a great story. The only reason I won’t be giving anyone this game for New Year is that the Epic Games Store doesn’t support gifting yet :-)

Observation
28 Jule 2019 ·

It’s the near future, and you are the artificial intelligence of a space station struck by disaster. Your systems are malfunctioning, most data is lost, and the crew is God knows where. You need to work out what happened and put things right.

That’s how No Code’s recent Observation starts. The game is fantastic. If you’re into sci-fi, especially the classics, Arthur C. Clarke and that kind of thing, you’ll love it. Of the games I’ve played recently in this genre, only SOMA is better.

Do yourself a favor: skip the reviews, because journalists can’t string two words together without spoilers. Skip the official trailer too. The game has a brilliant intro, and you should see it as part of the story, not on YouTube.

The intro’s music was written by Robin Finck of Nine Inch Nails, by the way. The visuals don’t make sense when you first see them, but once you work out what you’re looking at, they really get to you.

Yomawari: Night Alone
30 May 2019 ·

A lovely isometric exploration game about a town filled with classic Japanese ghosts and a girl looking for her missing sister. It’s not all that scary; mostly it keeps you on edge. Not all that hard either, though you do have to pay attention: there are lots of ghosts, they all behave differently, and you can’t afford to wander around daydreaming. It’s not all that fast, though you’ll need quick reflexes here and there, especially near the end.

I came away feeling really good about it. Maybe they didn’t have millions to spend, but you can tell they tried to make the best game they could.

Close to the Sun
14 May 2019 ·

Game journalists and YouTubers are competing to tear Close to the Sun apart in their reviews, but I unexpectedly liked it. It’s certainly no masterpiece, yet it captures the atmosphere of an enormous Flying Dutchman brilliantly. Parts of it bring BioShock to mind, but I’m not about to tear a story apart just because it reminds me of something else.

There’s also a fantastic single playing over the end credits, which literally sold me the game a couple of days ago. You can listen to it without touching the game itself.

I Do My Best
12 February 2019 ·

One thing that makes learning English much easier is practice that practically falls into your lap. Sometimes it’s more useful than examples from something like the Cambridge Dictionary, simply because it sticks better.

Yesterday in class, for example, we were working on hypothetical situations and getting our heads around wish and if only. Today, the first thing in my feed was this tweet. Hard to make it any clearer :-)

Or take the difference between try and trying, which we recently reviewed. I memorized it long ago thanks to Felicity’s strained “I do my best” in Borderlands: The Pre-Sequel. That scene sticks with you, and so does the sense that she just can’t do what’s being asked of her.
