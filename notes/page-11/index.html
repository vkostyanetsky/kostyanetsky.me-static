White Day
17 January 2018 ·

This horror game may be niche, but it really is rather good. The atmosphere of a school at night, filled with dark secrets, works brilliantly. Tension rarely lets up, and the game keeps surprising you where you least expect it. I’m an old hand at the genre and still jumped more than once.

The plot changes depending on the player’s actions, so reaching the end of the story takes more than one playthrough. I think it’s worth it. The antagonist alone is a good reason: you don’t often get one who’s more interesting, appealing, and tragic than the heroes and everyone else put together.

Exporting Bus Schedules
17 November 2017 ·

Ever since my colleagues and I redid the automation at KPAT, it has been running on Bus Terminal Management. It’s a 1C:Enterprise configuration, so hooking it up to pretty much anything is fairly quick.

First up, we wanted to send the bus schedules to E-Traffic and Yandex Schedules.

What Are These Services?

The first, E-Traffic, is Russia’s largest aggregator of bus terminal data. It collects information about scheduled departures and acts as an electronic ticket agent. Passengers don’t have to fight their way through individual bus terminal websites: E-Traffic lets them buy a ticket from anywhere to anywhere.

As for Yandex Schedules, it’s one of the Russian search engine’s side projects. Like E-Traffic, it’s mainly for passengers, and it can quickly put together fairly complicated routes with transfers. It doesn’t sell tickets, though.

How Did We Build the Export?

At first, we built a fairly elaborate setup to send schedules to both systems. We worked our asses off, until a conversation revealed that E-Traffic already knew how to pass schedules on to Yandex. Just like that, half the job disappeared.

On the technical side, we made a web service for Bus Terminal Management. E-Traffic sent a SOAP request, the service gathered the bus schedule data from the 1C:Enterprise infobase, and back came some XML.

We didn’t even have to write code to fetch schedules from every terminal in the KPAT network: all schedules are created and stored in one place, the main node of the distributed infobase, and that’s where we deployed the web service. In the end, coordination took longer than programming.

Where Can You See It?

The inner workings aren’t visible from outside, and there’s nothing unusual there anyway: robots talking to robots, big deal :-) The result of their chatter is much more interesting! For example, here’s today’s schedule from Kemerovo to Novokuznetsk.

Sebby x Player
14 August 2017 ·

I have translated Stardew Valley fan manga on a wave of enthusiasm at the end of July (SV is an enormously addictive farmer simulator which went out a couple of years ago).

It was an amusing experience. Firstly, an author of the manga is Chinese and her work was already translated from Chinese to English. I'm not sure what was the bigger problem — cultural differences or language skills, but several frames were a bit difficult to understand what's going on. Secondly, I've figured out that it's a real challenge to pick proper Russian words and not to get something like a quote from an official government's letter in the end.

So, a part of sense had lost during the process, I guess, although I tried to keep everything I could and even had a word with the author in order to solve the most problematic cases. It was quite funny — I don't know Chinese, she doesn't know Russian, so our a bit poor English was the only way to understand each other.

Something could have been translated better, I suppose, and several pictures definitely could have been changed more accurately. But you know what? Hell it out, perfection is a cruel mistress and I like the result yet. Yeah, there is no big deal or something like it — a drawing style is quite common, a plot has no significant differences with the game, but… I don't know. Perhaps, visual novels made a too sentimental son of a bitch out of me :-)

Thanks to Alice for helping me with image processing, Lem for helping me with the translation and Marika for the inspiration. And, sure, thanks to Quantus for the manga.

Layers of Fear
6 Jule 2017 ·

A mixed bag. On one hand, it does a vivid, colorful job of showing madness from the inside. The plot is generally decent, though at some point the ending becomes predictable.

On the other, it’s fantastically linear, with almost no challenge or logic to events. The story is told by a madman, inside his mind, and that leaves its mark on the gameplay: you can’t predict what you’re supposed to do in the next room.

So you wander around looking for triggers, trying to make sense of whatever weird thing comes next. A melting phone? Exploding clocks? Now and then, something really gets to you, like the letters between the hero and his wife. Those moments are rare, and they feel like a breath of fresh air.

Overall, I don’t regret playing it: it’s a sad, heavy story, with a lot of care put into the art and voice acting. But I suspect a lot of meaning drowned in its sea of symbolism.

Outlast 2
29 April 2017 ·

Not an outright failure, but very, very weak, especially compared with the first game or its DLC. There are barely any moments that scare you, stand out, or make you feel much of anything. And when one does come along, dying yet again soon wipes it out.

On the positive side: great voice acting, decent graphics, and good attention to small details. There are a dozen or so good scenes and half a dozen gorgeous shots that might make it worth playing. They’ve put some work into the world, though it mostly makes you feel sick and disgusted rather than scared, unlike Mount Massive.

Everything else is simply bad. Clumsy stealth in impenetrable darkness; endless scripted chases; bland characters we barely get to know; frequent bugs like floating in midair or enemies walking through objects.

To top it off, the ending is utterly empty. Yes, you can piece it together, and it all seems logical, but… How do I put this? After eight hours of running from psychopaths, you want something to show for it. Instead, you get an enormous, blazing, blinding pile of absolutely fuck-all, in every sense.

Firewatch
22 April 2017 ·

An ordinary, very human story, with something missing in a very human way. A spark, maybe? An idea? A point? The closer you get to the ending, the stronger the feeling that someone forgot to put soup in the pot.

I don’t mean it isn’t worth playing. The dialogue is very natural and beautifully written, and it does about three quarters of the work of drawing you in. The game also looks good, although I don’t quite understand all the raving reviews: something like The Vanishing of Ethan Carter had more impressive scenery. Overall, it’s a pleasure to play.

But the ending is frankly rushed, and there simply is no climax. Yes, I get what they’re going for: this is life, blah blah blah, more ellipses than full stops, blah blah blah. But come on! A story that asks a bunch of questions and answers none of them isn’t all that great, however much you tell me I need to be older to get it.

HPMoR
6 November 2016 ·

Just finished reading “Harry Potter and Methods of Rationality” — the epic fanfic based on the you-know-whose universe. An author is a tough specialist in the field of artificial intelligence and interesting on his own, but his book is what I insistently recommend to read.

First of all, it is the magnificent rethinking of the original saga. Many aspects seem not less fascinating, I may say; moreover, the plot itself is certainly excellent — I read two-thirds of the story without switching Gene Wilder off :-) Nevertheless, the author was skillful enough to make all points meet and finish a storyline in such a pretty way that I had almost nothing to complain about.

In any case, this book is a well way to meet with a lot of scientific and just interesting things which were mentioned along the way. The author is smart and his book is a smart one too.

Kholat
