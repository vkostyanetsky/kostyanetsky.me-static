A Principle of Non-Judgemental Attitude
2 September 2018 ·

There is a good note from Maxim Ilyahov about a way of analyzing something which doesn't concern you but annoys you anyway. It's reminded me of an American principle of non-judgemental attitude which I read about a couple of years ago. As for me, it uses different words to make the same point.

By the way, this principle presents in Russia too — there is even a famous adage “don't be judgemental so you won't be judged”. What a pity for us that it isn't so widespread yet.

These Nasty Smartphones
31 Marth 2018 ·

The artist is Adam Ellis.

As for me, it's a great illustration for an everlasting moan “why do you always stare at your smartphone blah blah blah”. A reason is quite simple: people have their friends, books, podcasts, games, and YouTube in their smartphones. It's much more fun than a frowning man or a grim women with guru syndrome next to them, for example.

The same is true of children. Social networks are full of arguments about it, but a reason is still the same: if a teenager flips through pictures in his phone in front of you, you bore him and a problem obviously is you.

Perfectly Unhappy
24 Marth 2017 ·

I came across How to Be Perfectly Unhappy, Matthew Inman’s comic about happiness, unhappiness, and what those words really mean.

The guy has a great sense of humor, which you’d expect from the creator of The Oatmeal. When people like that get serious, this is often what you get: something funny, wise, and, I don’t know, disarmingly honest.

Gear for My Level
13 Jule 2014 ·

I’ve updated my summer wardrobe a little. Minus the extra kilos I’d got so used to, I felt like an MMORPG character in the fitting room. I’d leveled up, and now I could wear better gear.

The problems are much the same, including the eternal question: “Should I buy this light armor set if I’m going to level up again soon?”

Bowling Pins on the Road
3 June 2013 ·

I wonder what percentage of pedestrians crossing wherever they please really struggle to reach a crosswalk, and what percentage simply don’t care about the consequences. I recently read Confessions of a Bowling Pin, Anton Buslov’s excellent post about crosswalks from the perspective of someone who is ill. But I still struggle to believe that all these well-fed people, who are hardly old, are too worn out to make it to the crossing.

It’s not so bad for me: on a bike, I can swerve easily and stop quickly. But I hate to think what it’s like for drivers and motorcyclists.

Sleep Well
17 April 2013 ·

Berezovsky is incredibly quiet at night. Big cities can’t sleep this deeply: there’s always someone wandering the streets, cars driving past, music playing, some dogs howling.

Here, not a soul. Just endless streetlights and sleepy traffic signals. And dead silence. Not a single sound, apart from the echo of my own footsteps.

The feeling is… Strange. As though I’d ended up in, I don’t know, Silent Hill or Pripyat, rather than a small mining town.
